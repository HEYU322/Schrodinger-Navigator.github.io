Schrödinger's Navigator performs zero-shot object navigation by generating three complementary, trajectory-conditioned 3D futures from left, right, and overhead virtual camera viewpoints. Their imagined cues are depth-aligned and combined in a Future-Aware Value Map (FAVM), complementing observed guidance with semantic target relevance, new free-space coverage, and geometric risk. The selected navigation target is executed by a local planner rather than by following imagined camera paths.

Teaser

Trajectory-conditioned 3D imagination lets the robot reason about what may exist behind an occluder before it commits to a route.

The key failure mode in real-world zero-shot object navigation is not only that the target may be hidden, but also that the hidden area may contain free space, clutter, or risk that the robot cannot infer from a single RGB-D snapshot. Schrödinger's Navigator models these unseen regions as multiple plausible futures and explicitly reasons over them.

Abstract

Paper Overview

Zero-shot object navigation (ZSON) requires robots to locate target objects in unseen environments without task-specific fine-tuning or pre-built maps. Severe occlusion and hidden risks can make single-scene reasoning brittle.

Our method samples left-bypass, right-bypass, and over-the-top virtual camera trajectories around a dominant occluder. FlashWorld generates hypothetical 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes conditioned on these trajectories; depth alignment and semantic transfer enable their evidence to be fused into a Future-Aware Value Map (FAVM).

The FAVM scores semantic target relevance, newly imagined free-space coverage, and geometric risk, then combines future guidance with observation-based navigation cues. The paper evaluates HM3D ObjectNav and controlled Unitree Go2 trials, showing comparable static-search performance and a clearer advantage under severe occlusion.

Key Ideas

What Changes Compared With Standard ZSON

1. Occluder-aware trajectory sampling

Using observed RGB-D geometry, the sampler constructs three complementary virtual camera paths around the dominant forward occluder: left, right, and overhead. These are used for imagination, not robot-executed motion.

2. Trajectory-conditioned 3D imagination

FlashWorld generates trajectory-conditioned 3D Gaussian Splatting scenes from the current observation. The predictions are scale-aligned using observed depth and fused as hypotheses of currently unseen space.

3. Future-aware waypoint selection

The FAVM combines target semantics, newly imagined free space, and geometric path-risk penalties, and is fused with observation-based guidance. A local planner converts the selected navigation target into executable waypoints; risk-aware ranking does not provide a formal safety guarantee.

Method

Framework

The pipeline receives a goal instruction, RGB-D observation, robot pose, and an accumulated map. An occluder-aware sampler generates three virtual camera trajectories (L/R/U), and a trajectory-conditioned 3D world model produces hypothetical 3D scenes, which are spatially aligned and semantically labeled before map fusion.

The FAVM combines semantic relevance, exploration, and geometric risk, and is fused with a normalized observation-based value map for target selection. The local planner computes executable waypoints from that navigation target. In HM3D, future imagination is refreshed every 50 observation updates; on the real robot, world-model inference is asynchronous.
